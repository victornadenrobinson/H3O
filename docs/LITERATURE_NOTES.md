# Literature notes (read from the PDFs, 2026-10-26)

## Wilson, Wong, Militzer, PRL 110, 151102 (2013): fcc vs bcc superionic water
**Result.** fcc oxygen sublattice has the lower Gibbs free energy than bcc under all conditions studied except 1 Mbar / 3000 K (difference about zero); transition predicted at 1.0 +- 0.5 Mbar. fcc has lower H mobility and perhaps a higher melting temperature. hcp O sublattice is not stable in MD (rapid distortion).

**Table I of the paper: G_bcc - G_fcc and its components, eV per H2O molecule (divide by 3 for per atom)**

| P (Mbar) | T (K) | dG | P dV | dU | -T dS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3000 | 0.002(11) | 0.017(16) | -0.037(20) | 0.023(28) |
| 2 | 3000 | 0.027(8) | 0.064(20) | 0.019(16) | -0.055(26) |
| 5 | 3000 | 0.074(7) | 0.072(26) | 0.087(13) | -0.084(30) |
| 10 | 5000 | 0.065(8) | 0.054(24) | 0.102(18) | -0.095(31) |
| 40 | 2000 | 0.256(8) | 0.198(14) | 0.229(10) | -0.165(19) |
| 40 | 3000 | 0.214(7) | 0.145(12) | 0.177(20) | -0.108(24) |
| 40 | 5000 | 0.180(11) | 0.033(19) | 0.188(18) | -0.042(28) |

Per atom at 3000 K: 200 GPa 9.0(2.7) meV, **500 GPa 24.7(2.3) meV**, 4000 GPa 71(2) meV. Linear interpolation in P between the 2 and 5 Mbar rows gives about 19 meV/atom at 400 GPa (crude).
P dV and dU favour fcc increasingly with pressure, -T dS (bcc has more entropy: H can use both tetrahedral and octahedral sites) opposes it. Check: dG = P dV + dU - T dS holds in every row.

**Method.** VASP, PBE, PAW, 900 eV, 2x2x2 k-points, NVT, timestep 0.2 fs, 2000-5000 steps per run; cubic cells with 32 (fcc), 54 (bcc), 48 (hcp) molecules (96/162/144 atoms). Volumes from the variable-cell method of Hernandez et al. Two-step coupling-constant integration:
1. DFT -> classical: two-body spline pair potentials fitted to the DFT-MD forces (force matching, Izvekov et al.) plus a harmonic one-body term holding O on lattice sites (spring 5 eV/A^2); 5 evenly spaced lambda, trapezoid rule (integrand nearly a straight line).
2. Classical -> reference: harmonic oscillators for O on lattice sites + noninteracting ideal gas for H; 48 lambda concentrated at small lambda (integrand strongly curved there), averages by classical Monte Carlo; halving the lambda points changed G_fcc - G_bcc negligibly.
Convergence: doubling the fcc cell (96 -> 182 atoms) or k-grid 2x2x2 -> 4x4x4 changed U by < 0.01 eV/molecule. Supplement Table II: 40 Mbar 3000 K energies per molecule fcc 32 mol 24.811(11) vs 64 mol 24.819(9); bcc 54 mol 25.006(10) vs 108 mol 24.987(6).
**Path test (supplement Table III):** integral computed directly (all potentials on at once) vs through two sub-steps (H-H and O-O first, then O-H), eV/molecule: bcc 1 Mbar 3000 K: 81.42(2) vs 81.51(3); fcc 40 Mbar 3000 K: 989.22(3) vs 989.09(3). The text says the difference is "on the order of 0.001 eV per molecule", but the printed numbers differ by 0.09 and 0.13 eV/molecule. Unresolved; do not rely on either statement.
**Supplement Table I (MD-averaged total energy, E_fcc - E_bcc, eV/molecule):** 2 Mbar 3000 K -0.076(11) [2x2x2 k], -0.052(11) [3x3x3]; 10 Mbar 5000 K -0.073(14), -0.053(18); 40 Mbar 3000 K -0.216(15), -0.200(10). These are energies, not free energies, and they do not obviously match the dU column of the main Table I (e.g. 2 Mbar: dU = +0.019(16) there). Unexplained.
**Table II (paper): H diffusion constants** fcc smaller than bcc at all densities (e.g. 5000 K, 11 g/cc: 2.48 bcc vs 1.33 fcc), stated in "A^2 fs^-1"; the magnitudes look scaled (a true 2.48 A^2/fs would be 0.25 cm^2/s), so treat the unit as unreliable.

## Soubiran and Militzer, ApJ 806, 228 (2015): H2O-H2 miscibility
Same group, same TI machinery: DFT -> classical non-bonding pair potentials fitted to DFT-MD forces (5-7 lambda points suffice, Fig. 2), then classical -> ideal gas reference with many lambda (classical runs are about 1e5 times faster than DFT). G = F + P V. 0.2 fs, PBE, 1100 eV, 2-70 GPa, 1000-6000 K. No fcc/bcc.

## Gupta, Stixrude, Schlichting (arXiv:2407.04685): H2-H2O miscibility
Direct DFT two-phase coexistence (PBEsol, gamma point, 500 eV, 300-450 atoms, 150-300 ps), critical curve up to 2000 GPa, no H3O molecules found in the fluid. No MLP and no free-energy method for solids; not used for the fcc/bcc work. Possibly relevant for the H-water hull.

## Cheng et al. data set (the authors' public repository) and the two Nature Communications papers (read from the uploaded PDFs, 2026-10-26)
**Repository** https://github.com/BingqingCheng/superionic-water (data set of Cheng, Bethkenhagen, Pickard, Hamel, Nat. Phys. 17, 1228 (2021); cloned read-only outside this repo). Contents: `chemical-potential/` (mu_bcc, mu_fcc, mu_Pbcm, mu_liquid on a grid of 80 pressures 10-800 GPa x 60 temperatures 300-5500 K, in eV per formula unit = per H2O molecule: `mu-mlp-cl-*` classical MLP level, `mu-mlp2pbe-*` correction mu_PBE - mu_MLP by free-energy perturbation with its error, `mu-nqe-*` nuclear quantum effects; PBE+NQE = sum of the three), `training-set/` (N2P2 format, atomic units Hartree/bohr), `nnp/` (network), `vasp-pbe-example/`, `nnp-md-example/`, `phase-boundary/`, `diffusion-coefficient/`, `EOS/`, example structures of bcc/fcc/hcp/mixed superionic phases.
**mu_bcc - mu_fcc, eV per molecule (positive = fcc more stable), PBE-corrected, classical nuclei, interpolated to 3000 K:** 100 GPa +0.0038(18); 200 GPa +0.0314(20); 300 GPa +0.0527(19); 400 GPa +0.0662(17) (= 22.1(0.6) meV/atom); 500 GPa +0.0794(16) (= 26.5(0.5) meV/atom). Quoted errors are the statistical error of the PBE correction only. **Wilson et al. DFT at 3000 K:** +0.002(11), +0.027(8), +0.074(7) at 100, 200, 500 GPa: the two independent calculations (DFT-TI with 32/54-molecule cells vs a neural-network potential with FEP correction) agree within 1 sigma of Wilson's error bars. NQE change the difference by < 0.002 eV/molecule at 3000 K. At 2000 K the bcc phase is the more stable one below about 340 GPa and fcc above (in that table); at 5000 K the tabulated range is 280-540 GPa. Derived table: `docs/data/cheng2021_dmu_bcc_fcc.csv` (script `docs/extract_cheng_mu.py`); both sources are plotted in `docs/figures/dg_fcc_bcc_vs_P.png`.
**Their potential and training set:** a Behler-Parrinello network, 2 hidden layers of 20 nodes (`nnp/input.nn`), Kalman-filter training, 200 epochs, trained FROM SCRATCH; the training-set files contain 17,516 structure entries in total (about 3.5 million atoms; typical cells 162 atoms, some 243-378; the "all-committee-*" files may overlap the others), covering 300-15000 K. For comparison our fine-tune uses 747 frames. Force scale of that data: per-component force RMS 4-5.4 eV/A at high P and T (1.7 at 2000 K, 2.3 at 300 K low P), so our 0.73 eV/A validation RMSE is about 14% of the force scale.
**DFT settings of that data set (`vasp-pbe-example`):** PBE, ENCUT 1000 eV, PREC High, Fermi smearing ISMEAR -1 with SIGMA = 0.2585 eV (kT at 3000 K, as in our data), POTIM 0.25 fs, NBANDS 280, a single Baldereschi mean-value k-point (0.25, 0.25, 0.25), NSW 50000.

**Reinhardt, Bethkenhagen, Coppari, Millot, Hamel, Cheng, Nat. Commun. 13, 4707 (2022), "Thermodynamics of high-pressure ice phases explored with atomistic simulations"** (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32374-1; data in https://github.com/BingqingCheng/highP-ice): insulating bcc ices VII/VII'/X are one thermodynamic phase, VII'' superionic has a first-order boundary with VII'; X -> Pbcm near 300 GPa (bifurcation, no barrier), phase diagram below 100 GPa and X vs Pbcm up to 800 GPa. No fcc-bcc numbers. METHOD (relevant to us): chemical potentials by Hamiltonian TI from a harmonic reference crystal (Hessian from phonons, i-PI) to the MLP, then Gibbs-Duhem integration along isotherms and Gibbs-Helmholtz integration along isobars; consistency checked by several independent TI routes (isotherms at 100/300/600/1000 K, along T 100-2000 K, along P 100-800 GPa) and by direct coexistence; errors from 95% confidence bands of polynomial fits; the MLP result is promoted to PBE by adding mu - mu^MLP computed with the **free-energy perturbation method** (this is the correction discussed earlier; they also say the MLP residual error is partly due to missing long-range electrostatics); nuclear quantum effects by path-integral MD (64 formula units, 32 beads), contribution to the chemical-potential difference < 5 meV per molecule. VASP 5.4, ENCUT 1000 eV, k-spacing 0.3 /A. MLP MD: LAMMPS NPT, 128-2048 molecules, 0.5 fs. Metadynamics for the solid-solid transition. They also state that superionic fcc (ice XVIII) and superionic hcp have similar chemical potentials and are both stable between about 100 and 800 GPa (their ref. 18 = the 2021 Nat. Phys. paper).

**Andriambariarijaona et al., Nat. Commun. 17, 374 (2025), "Observation of a mixed close-packed structure in superionic water"** (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67063-2; source data https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.30624377; EuXFEL data https://doi.org/10.22003/XFEL.EU-DATA-004463-00, embargo until 2026-10-30): ultrafast XRD of multiply shock-compressed water up to about 180 GPa. Above 150 GPa and about 2500 K the patterns need a mixed close-packed (fcc/hcp stacking disorder, hcp probability alpha 0.25-0.32) superionic phase rather than pure fcc; around 20-100 GPa and 2000-2500 K bcc and fcc signatures coexist (their Fig. 5 shows the mu_bcc = mu_fcc region of Cheng et al. as a hatched area near 25-110 GPa, 1600-2500 K). Simulations used the same Cheng potential (n2p2 in LAMMPS, trained on the PBE data set of Cheng et al.), up to 13,824 molecules, 100 ps NPT at 0.25 fs, XRD computed from snapshots. No free energies.
